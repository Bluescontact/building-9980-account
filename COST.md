# The Cost Account — who the correction protected, and who paid for it

*Kevin's mark, September 1, 2026. This account is kept apart from the route floor by
design: whether the route functioned is one question; who carried the stop is another,
and mixing them hands any institution a wage dispute to contest the route with. Kept
apart is not the same as untold. This is the telling. No dollar figure is carried —
a figure is a handle. Durations and events are carried, dated, at their source's
discount.*

## 1. The shape of it, in one paragraph

A worker performed unpermitted work in a permit-required confined space for roughly two
months (the hoistway, from the start of work through the Feb 25, 2025 restriction). When
he raised the concern, he lost income. He reported safety twice — the Jan 23, 2025
written refusal and the Feb 25, 2025 restriction — and lost income both times. The
correction that followed protected every party on that jobsite: the site got its
classification, the general contractor got its permit posture, the employer got its
compliance, the apprentice got a safer hoistway. The one party the correction did not
protect was the reporting worker. The cost of producing the correction was routed to
him — without any seat deciding it should be, which is precisely the finding: routed
without a decision.

## 3. What the distribution shows

Run the cost-routing diagnostic on the table above: the same cost appears repeatedly in
column two — *the reporter pays* — and the same empty answer sits where a routing decision
should be. No document in the record shows any seat deciding the reporter should carry
the cost of the correction. No document shows any seat deciding he shouldn't. The cost
was not assigned; it defaulted. That is the structural finding of the whole record,
stated at the personal layer: **a correction everyone else was protected by was produced
at the sole expense of the person who reported the need for it — twice — and the second
worker it touched (the apprentice) was made whole only to the extent the reporter paid
him personally.**

The Feb 28 email is the exception that proves it: the one written employer response says
the hours are "not 'lost work'" and "will thus be paid for." That sentence is the record
admitting the cost should not land on the worker. What the record does not contain is any
document showing it didn't.

## 4. What this account does and does not do

It does not assert a claim. The May 21, 2025 waiver covers the union and the employer
(and only them — not the GC, the site, or DOE). It does not carry a figure. It does not
enter the route floor: the disclosure's four-record floor stands on its own, and no seat
gets to answer the route question with a wage argument.

What it does: it completes the account. The eviction and the year without a floor are the
personal layer running the same grammar as the institutional one — a cost landing on the
giver with no seat, no entry, and no disposition. Ledger C territory: the least-developed
card in the system, and the one this page begins.

## 5. Falsifier

A payroll record showing the restriction period was paid as the Feb 28 email promised;
a record of any party reimbursing the apprentice's wages other than the reporter. Either
narrows this account the day it appears, and it will be restated to say so.
