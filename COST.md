# The Cost Account — who the correction protected, and who paid for it

*Kevin's mark, September 1, 2026; corrected September 28, 2026 (Version 3.1 — each
correction is marked, and the superseded wording is kept in the notes). This account is
kept apart from the route floor by design: whether the route functioned is one question;
who carried the stop is another, and mixing them hands any institution a wage dispute to
contest the route with. Kept apart is not the same as untold. This is the telling.
~~No dollar figure is carried — a figure is a handle. Durations and events are carried,
dated, at their source's discount.~~ Corrected Sep 28, 2026: hours are carried, with
their sources. A figure is still a handle, and no figure here is a demand; the few dollar

— which is why they live here, at their stated discount, and not in the route floor.*

**The point is not the hours.** The question is whether a worker could refuse unsafe work
without being penalized for refusing. Kone's own policy says "No form of retribution or
intimidation shall be directed at any employee for exercising their right to refuse
unsafe work provided that the individual follows required procedures." The hours are
the evidence of that question, not the claim.

## 1. The shape of it, in one paragraph

*Corrected Sep 28, 2026; the Version 3 paragraph is kept in the note below.*

A worker performed work in a hoistway that Sandia's industrial hygienist had classified
as a permit-required confined space on December 24, 2024 — on his own account, roughly
two months of work before February 25, 2025. His supervisor was copied on the
classification; he received it on February 20, 2025. He reported safety twice — the Jan
23, 2025 written refusal and the Feb 25, 2025 restriction. Both cost him work. He was
never docked and nobody sent him home: the crew worked ten-hour days, and with no
alternative work and no word on how to proceed he left after eight. The hours between are
the loss. The correction that followed protected the parties going forward: the site got
its classification question answered on the record, the employer its compliance posture,
the permit question posed and assigned. The reporter it did not protect — nor his
apprentice, who lost hours too; the reporter paid half of the apprentice's union
initiation dues, about $500, roughly what the apprentice had lost in wages. The cost of
producing the correction was routed to the reporting side without any seat deciding it
should be. Seats with authority to decide existed — 10 CFR 708's Office of Hearings and
Appeals; the OIG under 41 U.S.C. §4712 — and no record shows the question was ever put
to them. Whether the route to them was ever given to this worker is a question he is
asking, not a finding (see the README). The cost has not been assigned; so far, it has
defaulted. That default is dated, and it is still open to any seat that decides.

## 2a. The mechanism, corrected (Kevin's statement, September 25, 2026, at the giver's discount; entered on his instruction — "This point deserves correction")

The account's "income lost" rows compressed a mechanism the giver has now stated
plainly, and the corrected form is narrower and stronger than the compressed one. He
was not docked by the employer; he was **allowed to stay on the clock without the
ability to produce forward momentum on the project.** During the periods of safety
delay — on his account, about a week of sitting idle before refusing to operate for
the pest-control inspection he had been told was a hazmat situation, and roughly three
weeks of delay after the confined-space restriction — no sufficient alternative work
was assigned. NEBA Art. XXVIII Par. 4(b) names alternative work as what a good-faith
refusal is met with; the record holds no document showing it was provided. Faced with
ten-hour days of idleness, he chose to leave after eight; the hours between are the
loss, and the choosing is part of the cost, not a waiver of it. His own words, entered
verbatim with their ambiguity held rather than resolved: "The effect i lost from
recolection 12 hours to get an approval for a bottle of lysol" — whether the twelve
hours counts the loss or the approval delay is his to fix; the approval request
itself is now documented (the Jan 16, 2025, 6:37 PM MST "Request for Basic Safety
Tool Access" for a 10:1 bleach solution, in hand). Falsifier, unchanged in kind:
timekeeping or dispatch records for the periods; either narrows this section the day
it appears.

*Added Sep 28, 2026:* the ambiguity above is now fixed by the giver. The twelve hours
are the hours lost over the week after the rodent report, as his own Feb 25, 2025 email
counts them ("twelve hours following rodent hazard reporting"). The approval was verbal,
for a CDC-listed disinfectant (Lysol) — he bought six bottles, not one ("a bottle of
lysol" was shorthand), did the cleanup himself, and Kone reimbursed him.

**And the employer named the question first.** The Sep 25, 2026 mailbox pass
recovered Kone's written reply of Jan 20, 2025 — five days before the restriction:
"We understand the additional safety training needed to preform this work, but **we
need to establish who will be responsible for these additional cost and delays.**"
The cost-assignment question this account finds unanswered was posed, in writing, by
the employer itself, at the start — and no record shows it was ever established by
anyone.

## 4. What this account does and does not do

It does not assert a claim. The May 21, 2025 waiver covers the union and the employer
(and only them — not the GC, the site, or DOE). ~~It does not carry a figure.~~ *Corrected
Sep 28, 2026: it carries hours, and the author's own dollar amounts, as evidence, not as
a bill.* It does not
enter the route floor: the disclosure's four-record floor stands on its own, and no seat
gets to answer the route question with a wage argument. Declining the wage routes that
exist — a state wage claim on the Feb 28 promise; the still-open §4712 window — is the
giver's choice, owned as a choice, consistent with the waiver's spirit and the account's
form: it records; it does not demand.

What it does: it completes the account. The loss of housing and the time since *(wording
corrected Sep 28, 2026; the earlier words about the housing loss are withdrawn)* are the
personal layer running the same grammar as the institutional one — a cost landing on the
giver with no seat, no entry, and no disposition. The record shows a sequence: reports,
lost hours, resignation, housing loss, retirement withdrawal. It does not prove the
reports caused the rest, and the hardship is not raised as a claim against anyone.
