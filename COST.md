# The Cost Account — who the correction protected, and who paid for it

*Kevin's mark, September 1, 2026. This account is kept apart from the route floor by
design: whether the route functioned is one question; who carried the stop is another,
and mixing them hands any institution a wage dispute to contest the route with. Kept
apart is not the same as untold. This is the telling. No dollar figure is carried —
a figure is a handle. Durations and events are carried, dated, at their source's
discount. Rows resting on the giver's statement alone say so, and carry no external
falsifier by nature — which is why they live here, at their stated discount, and not in
the route floor.*

## 1. The shape of it, in one paragraph

A worker performed work in a hoistway that was later restricted pending permit-required
confined space classification — on his own account, roughly two months of it; whether the
space was permit-required during that period is the question the site has not answered.
When he raised the concern, he lost income, as reported. He reported safety twice — the
Jan 23, 2025 written refusal and the Feb 25, 2025 restriction. The first cost him income;
the second stopped his work for a period whose compensation is not established. The
correction that followed protected the parties going forward: the site got its
classification question answered on the record, the employer its compliance posture, the
permit question posed and assigned. The reporter it did not protect — nor his apprentice,
whose lost wages the reporter repaid by half from his own pocket, on his own account. The
cost of producing the correction was routed to the reporting side without any seat
deciding it should be. Seats with authority to decide existed — 10 CFR 708's Office of
Hearings and Appeals; the OIG under 41 U.S.C. §4712 — and none was ever put the question,
because the route to them was never transmitted. The cost has not been assigned; so far,
it has defaulted. That default is dated, and it is still open to any seat that decides.

## 3. What the distribution shows

Run the cost-routing diagnostic on the table above: the same cost appears repeatedly in
column two — *the reporting side pays* — and the same empty answer sits where a routing
decision should be. No document in the record shows any seat deciding the reporter should
carry the cost of the correction. No document shows any seat deciding he shouldn't. The
cost was not assigned; it defaulted — to the reporter, and to the apprentice until the
reporter personally repaid half. A correction the other parties were protected by was
produced at the expense of the people who reported the need for it.

The one written employer response says the hours are "not 'lost work'" and "will thus be
paid for." No document in the record shows they were. That gap — between the employer's
own sentence and the absence of the payroll record that would close it — is the cost
account's whole case, and either document ends it.

## 4. What this account does and does not do

It does not assert a claim. The May 21, 2025 waiver covers the union and the employer
(and only them — not the GC, the site, or DOE). It does not carry a figure. It does not
enter the route floor: the disclosure's four-record floor stands on its own, and no seat
gets to answer the route question with a wage argument. Declining the wage routes that
exist — a state wage claim on the Feb 28 promise; the still-open §4712 window — is the
giver's choice, owned as a choice, consistent with the waiver's spirit and the account's
form: it records; it does not demand.

What it does: it completes the account. The eviction and the year without a floor are the
personal layer running the same grammar as the institutional one — a cost landing on the
giver with no seat, no entry, and no disposition.

## 5. Falsifiers

Row by row, above. The two that would do the most work: a payroll record showing the
restriction period was paid as the Feb 28 email promised, and a record of any party other
than the reporter reimbursing the apprentice's wages. Either narrows this account the day
it appears, and it will be restated to say so.
