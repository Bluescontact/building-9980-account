## The finding, in full

*Corrected Sep 28, 2026. This section was rewritten; the Version 3 wording is kept in
full in the note at the end of the section.*

The two parts below are the two faces of the sentence above. The cost is what goes
unassigned when action happens without accounting. The route is a question: whether the
accounting channel — DOE's Employee Concerns Program — was ever given to this worker. The
acting channel worked.

A subcontractor's elevator mechanic on a DOE site worked a hoistway that Sandia's
industrial hygienist had classified as a permit-required confined space on December 24,
2024, for electrical and mechanical hazards, with a path to reclassification once those
were eliminated. His supervisor was copied on that classification; he did not receive it
until February 20, 2025. He reported safety twice in writing: a refusal on January 23,
2025, and a stop-work restriction on February 25, 2025.

The hours: before February 25 he lost 12 hours after the rodent report and 14 after a
site-ordered sewer-line shutdown. Of those 26 hours the employer's February 28 email said
they were "not 'lost work'… will thus be paid for"; no payment or further answer
followed. During the confined-space delay that began February 25 he kept working outside
the hoistway, about 32 hours a week, and every hour he worked was paid; the loss was the
gap to the crew's ten-hour days, 24 or 32 hours (his May 2025 binder says 32). In all, 50
to 58 hours, none of it paid. The point is not the hours. It is whether a worker could
refuse unsafe work without being penalized for refusing.

The correction that followed his reports protected the parties going forward: the site,
the general contractor, the employer. It did not protect the reporter, nor his
apprentice, who lost hours too; the reporter paid half of the apprentice's union
initiation dues out of his own pocket, about $500, roughly what the apprentice had lost
in wages. No decision assigning that cost to the reporter appears in any record reviewed
or returned by any seat. Seats with authority to decide it existed — 10 CFR 708's Office
of Hearings and Appeals; the OIG under 41 U.S.C. §4712, whose three-year window has not
yet run — and no record shows the question was ever put to them. The cost has not been
assigned; so far, it has defaulted. On his own account, entered at that discount: he was
put out of his housing without notice on July 25, 2025, and has lived since in a bus. He
withdrew the part of his retirement he could reach, about $40,000; the rest, about
$142,000 in union annuity money, is locked until retirement age. His September 2026
hardship request was rejected ("No funds available"), and he is appealing to the plan's
Trustees, who meet in December (see [annuity/](annuity/)). The crisis is ongoing. See
[COST.md](COST.md).

Underneath is a question about the route. He cited OSHA because OSHA was the only name he
had. The operative authority on a DOE site is 10 CFR 851, and DOE has its own route for
worker concerns, the Employee Concerns Program (DOE O 442.1B). He does not remember being
given that route — in site orientation, in badging, or by the employer, the general
contractor, or the Local — and can find no record of it. That is a question he is asking,
not a finding. The records that would settle it are Sandia's training record for his
badge, the content of the ES&H Awareness course (ESH100) as assigned in 2024, and the
NNSA records requested under FOIA 26-00666-PV.

DOE's May 2022 assessment of work planning and control at SNL-NM recorded: "Interviews
confirmed that NTESS Center 4700 construction managers did not fully understand DOE's WSH
jurisdiction" (p. 11). It also found that the Sandia Field Office's Employee Concerns
Program had not documented two case files in sufficient detail, or correctly documented
and communicated their final dispositions (deficiency D-SFO-1, p. 19, discussed p. 16).
The open question is whether that understanding had reached the people running
subcontracted work by 2025. The 2022 report proves nothing about 2025, and this account
does not use it that way.

Thirteen venues tested, January 2025 through September 2026; every one that answered,
answered with a jurisdiction or a procedure. In all that time one produced a case number
— the OIG's, 25-0737-C, confirmed opened and already closed in the same email (Dec 29,
2025; the email is in hand, recovered from the author's Gmail Trash on Sep 28, 2026) —
and one produced a disposition: on September 1, 2026, the third day after the account
was published to every seat at once, the OIG closed the complaint in writing, declining
to state the date of closure, its basis, or whether the safety allegation was ever
referred. The three questions asked in writing on August 31 stand open as the
disposition's residue. The shape of the entry is the exhibit.

from the working tree on September 3, remained readable in prior commits. On September 5

exception serves: no third party should hold, through this repository's history, what its

the author. Commit identifiers before this date changed as a consequence and any external
