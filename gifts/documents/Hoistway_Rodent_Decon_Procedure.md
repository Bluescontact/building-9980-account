## 1. Recognition — before you open up

Signs of rodent activity a mechanic will actually see: droppings on sills, headers,
door tracks, pit floors, controller tops; nesting material (shredded insulation, paper,
fabric) in pits and on car tops; urine staining (dark streaks, ammonia smell in a closed
hoistway). In hantavirus-endemic areas (the Southwest — New Mexico, Arizona, Colorado,
Utah — and rural sites generally), treat any droppings as potentially infectious.

## 2. Assessment — spray-and-wait, or stop-and-call

**Spray-and-wait (this procedure covers it):** scattered droppings on surfaces you must
touch or work near; light, localized contamination; no active nest, no carcasses in
number, no HVAC involvement.

**Stop-and-call (this procedure does not cover it):** active nesting; dead rodents more
than [IH: ___]; contamination heavier than [IH: ___ / describe]; droppings inside
equipment enclosures that must be opened energized; any contamination in air-handling
paths; any worker symptoms. Stop, restrict access, notify supervision and the Safety
Representative in writing, and request qualified remediation.

## 3. The method — wet, never dry

**See it. Spray it with disinfectant. Leave it undisturbed until it's wet through.
No dry sweeping. No vacuum. No compressed air.**

1. Ventilate the space if possible; wait 30 minutes before entry where the space has
   been closed.
2. Don PPE (Section 4) before disturbing anything.
3. Spray droppings, nesting, and stained surfaces with disinfectant — a 10% bleach
   solution (1 part household bleach : 9 parts water, mixed fresh) or an EPA-registered
   disinfectant — until soaked through. Soak minimum **5 minutes** (or the disinfectant
   label's contact time, whichever is longer).
4. Wipe up with paper towels. Do not sweep or scrape dry material at any point.
5. Bag the towels, gloves, and debris; seal the bag; dispose per site direction.
6. Disinfect the cleaned surface a second time. Disinfect or discard any tools that
   contacted the material.
7. Wash hands and forearms with soap and water immediately after de-gloving.

## 4. PPE — donning and doffing order

- Respirator: half-face elastomeric or N100/P100 filtering facepiece, fit-tested.
  [IH: confirm minimum respirator class for light decon vs. heavy contamination.]
- Gloves: nitrile under work gloves if abrasion is expected. Glove sequence: don nitrile
  last, doff first; peel first glove into the second; bag both.
- Eye protection where spraying overhead.
- Doffing order: gloves → wash → respirator → wash again. Bag disposables with the debris.
